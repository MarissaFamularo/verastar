How to set up Google Scholar alerts

A Google Scholar alert is a saved search that emails you new papers as Scholar finds them. Setting one up takes a minute. Keeping it useful takes a little more thought, so this guide covers both.

THE SHORT ANSWER

Search for your topic on scholar.google.com, click the envelope icon in the left sidebar of the results, confirm your email address, and click Create alert. Scholar will email you newly published papers that match the search.

Create your first alert

  1. Run the search you want to follow. Put multi-word concepts in quotes, such as "carotid stenosis", so the alert matches the phrase rather than either word.
  2. Check the first page of results. The alert will send more of whatever you see here. If it is already drifting off topic, add a second required term or exclude a frequent stray word with a minus sign, like -veterinary, and search again.
  3. Click the envelope icon in the left sidebar of the results page.
  4. Confirm the email address and click Create alert. Any address works. If it is not your signed-in Google account, Scholar sends a verification link first, and the alert starts once you click it.
  5. Manage it later from the Alerts page. Open the Scholar menu and choose Alerts to see, edit, or cancel every alert you have.

Three alerts worth adding

A topic alert is the obvious one. These catch what a topic search misses.

New papers citing a key study

Find the trial or review that defines your area, click Cited by under it, then click the envelope icon above the list of citing articles. Every new paper that cites it comes to you, whatever words it uses.

New work from a specific author

If the author has a Google Scholar profile, open it and click Follow. If not, create an alert on author:"AW Bradbury". For a common name, add a topic word or an institution to keep it from catching namesakes.

Citations of your own papers

Set up a Google Scholar profile, then click Follow on it and choose new citations. You hear when your work is cited, which is useful for tracking a paper's reach and for spotting replies to it.

Where a Scholar alert stops

An alert matches words. It does not know which papers matter to you, and it does not read them. A busy topic can bury a quiet one in the same inbox, and the numbers in each abstract are yours to check against the paper.

That gap is what Verastar is built for. It searches PubMed once per topic, so a crowded field cannot crowd out a small one. It scores each new paper against what you told it to watch for, keeps only the few that clear the bar, and checks each reported value against the source text. A value it cannot match is withheld and labeled, not passed along.

Common questions

Do I need a Google account for Google Scholar alerts?

No. You can enter any email address. If it is not your signed-in Google account, Scholar emails a verification link, and the alert starts once you click it.

How do I get an alert when a paper is cited?

Find the paper in Google Scholar, click Cited by under it, then click the envelope icon on the list of citing articles.

How do I follow new papers by one author?

Click Follow on the author's Scholar profile, or create an alert on a search such as author:"AW Bradbury", adding a topic word if the name is common.

Why is my alert sending papers I don't want?

The alert is a saved search, so it matches words, not relevance. Quote multi-word concepts, add a second required term, or exclude a frequent off-topic word with a minus sign, then recreate the alert.

How do I stop a Google Scholar alert?

Open the Scholar menu, choose Alerts, and click Cancel next to the alert. Each alert email also has a cancel link.
